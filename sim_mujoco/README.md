# sim_mujoco

The MuJoCo simulation node. It stands any number of robots: each joins by attaching over the `simulation_robot` contract, naming the copy it runs as, the model it is and where it stands, and the engine composes the scene from the models standing in it, each attached under a prefix of its own name at its own placement. A robot joining or leaving composes and compiles the scene again, and the robots that stay carry their joint positions, their speeds, their actuators' targets and the activations of the actuators that integrate (a wheel's rate loop) onto it, along with the engine's clock. The viser viewer is served on `viewer_port` (23818) for as long as the node runs, and the node announces it as its `viewer` page, so a launch lists its URLs. Each scene is drawn on it as the scene is composed, with or without a robot in it, and a browser open on it sees the scene composed next without connecting again. A viewer whose port another process holds serves on one the operating system picks.

A robot that asks for no placement takes the next free spot on a square lattice of 1.5 m, the lattice Isaac Sim parks robots on, so a fleet stands alike on both. A placement within 1.5 m of a robot standing, or of one admitted and still on its way in, is refused naming that robot: two robots closer together than the lattice leaves them resolve their overlap by throwing each other. A robot with a base rolls: it hangs from the world on a free joint named as the robot is, its placement sets where it starts across the floor and the heading it starts at, it settles onto the floor the scene lays for every robot that rolls, and from there its wheels carry it, gripping the floor and slipping where the floor lets them. Its placement follows its base on the state rate: where its wheels carried it, facing the heading they turned it to, which the spacing rule and the scene composed next take. Any other robot holds where it is placed. A robot stands under the name of the copy it runs as, and that name carries no `/`, which is what stands between a robot's name and the name of anything it carries, and is not `world`, the name of the scene's own body, which a robot that rolls names its body by.

The engine plays the follower role of every standing robot's limb pairings and the camera role of their rendered cameras. Nothing in it is about one robot: what it knows of a model is data.

MuJoCo drops a model's own `<option>` and `<statistic>` when it is attached and steps it under the scene's, so the scene takes the settings its models share, and a model asking for others is refused, naming both models and the settings they disagree on.

## Slots

| Slot | Pairing | Holds |
|---|---|---|
| `arms` | `joint_link`, follower | one pair per arm of the standing robot |
| `grippers` | `gripper_link`, follower | one pair per gripper |
| `bases` | `joint_link`, follower | one pair per base: the wheels of a robot that rolls |
| `rgb_cameras` | `sim_rgb_camera_link`, camera | one pair per colour camera a relay views |
| `rgbd_cameras` | `sim_rgbd_camera_link`, camera | one pair per depth camera a relay views |

A pair's robot is the copy it carries. A limb pair's limb is the link it comes from on the robot's side, so a backbone names its downstream links after its limbs and a launcher reads `left_arm: "simulation_inst/arms"` for an OpenArm and `arm: "simulation_inst/arms"` for an SO-101; a base pair's base is the link its base node pairs it from, `base: "simulation_inst/bases"`. A camera pair's camera is its relay's name in the copy (`wrist_left`, `wrist`). The reading is [`sim_robot_core`](https://github.com/Peppy-bot/public-peppy-libs/tree/main/sim_robot_core)'s, which the Isaac Sim node shares.

A robot keeps its place while its pairs are its model's: a pair for every limb and the base the model has and none for a limb, a base or a camera it lacks. A camera the model has may go unpaired, and is then not rendered. A robot whose pairs differ for its pair grace time (`robot_pair_grace_ms`) is taken out, and its goal fails with `resource_unavailable`, naming both lists. A robot is ready once it holds a pair for every limb and the base of its own model.

A joint setpoint is taken as `sim_robot_core.setpoints` parses it: one position per joint of the arm, or per wheel of the base, left then right, no velocities or one per joint, each a number from -1e10 to 1e10, the largest MuJoCo holds. One the parse refuses is dropped as it arrives, and the log says why. An arm's servos follow the positions of its latest setpoint. A base's wheels follow the velocities of its latest setpoint, as the base's driver runs them in profile velocity mode, rate 0 for a setpoint that carries none, and the positions go unread; its `joint_states` carry the wheels' measured angles and rates back, with no efforts, as an arm's do. A base's setpoint belongs to its pair: once the engine's reading of its pairs, every quarter of the pair grace time, finds that pair gone, the wheels are held where they stand, the integral of each wheel's rate loop cleared, at rate 0, and a pair that comes back drives nothing until its leader sends. A robot whose stay ends takes its setpoints with it, so a copy that joins again under its name drives nothing until its own leaders send. A part whose setpoints the parse refuses is logged once, until one of them fits.

Each wheel runs the rate loop of its model's MJCF: a `<velocity>` actuator, its `kv` the loop's proportional gain, and an `<intvelocity>` actuator, its `kp` the loop's integral gain, whose activation is the angle the target rates carry the wheel to, both of gear 1 and capped together by the joint's `actuatorfrcrange`. The engine clamps each loop's integral at what that cap holds, its activation within the cap over its `kp` of the wheel's angle, as a drive clamps its integral at its current limit, so a stalled wheel that is let go runs on by no more than that angle. A wheel driven otherwise, a wheel that is not a hinge, whose actuator force no `actuatorfrcrange` caps, or whose `<intvelocity>` clamps its activation with an `actrange`, is refused as the robot stands, naming the wheel and what its MJCF needs.

An `attach` goal's `placement` is a pose in `world`, the one frame of the simulation, which a placement with no frame names, whose orientation turns about +z alone; its timestamp, given or left out, places it in `world` at any time. The goal is refused with `unknown_frame` for a placement in another frame, with `rejected` for a model with no entry, a name the scene cannot stand a robot under, or a placement that the shared parse of `control_core_py.geometry` refuses or that turns about another axis, with `busy` for the robot of the caller while it still joins, and with `precondition_failed` for a name or a spot another robot holds, a second robot of the same caller, or another model for the robot the caller stands. A goal refused with `busy` takes the robot over once it stands, so `robot_initializer` sends it again; any other refusal stays until someone changes the scene. A robot that leaves gives its name back as soon as its stay ends, before the scene lets it go, so a copy that joins straight back is never refused for it. A cancel of its caller takes the robot out and ends the goal cancelled. The sender grace of `attach` is the pair grace time: when the process that sent the goal is gone, the robot stays for that time, and then peppy cancels the goal and the robot leaves. A caller that comes back within it attaches the robot that still stands, and the goal that held the robot ends cancelled, saying that another goal of the same caller holds the robot now. The engine fails the goal with `resource_unavailable` when it takes the robot out itself: the pair grace time ran out, the caller could not be told that the robot stands, or the simulation stops. A scene that cannot stand the robot fails the goal with `attempt_failed`.

## Models

A model is two entries of the same name and its MuJoCo model. `sim_robot_core` builds what a robot of the model is made of, whatever the engine, from the description its attach goal carries, its URDF and SRDF: its limbs under the names the robot answers to, the joints each moves, the wheels of its base, the differential base its description gives, where each finger closes and opens, its cameras at their optical frames, each picture's header naming the camera's frame, the optical frame the description gives the camera's name (the link `<name>_optical_frame`, which sensor_mounts gives for it), its capture time and its `capture_index`, which a colour and a depth picture of one capture share, and the posture it starts in, every arm at its home state with its gripper closed. An attach whose description the engine's entry for the model does not fit is refused with `rejected`.

The MuJoCo model of every robot is the release of Waldo's catalogue that `sim_base_images/waldo_catalogue.py` pins, which the base image bakes under `PEPPY_ROBOT_ASSETS_DIR` (`/opt/robot_assets`): each robot's pinned tree with the files the release lays over it, and the release's `robots.json`. Waldo stands its robots from the same release, so both engines stand one model of each robot. From each robot's entry of `robots.json` the engine reads (`engine/catalogue_robots.py`):

| Key | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `wrapper` | the robot's MJCF, over its tree; the scene attaches the body of it that carries the robot, and the floor, the light and the keyframe the file holds beside that body stay behind. A robot with no wrapper is attached from its `source.scene` |
| `mount.body` | the body that carries the whole robot |
| `mount.stance` | `rolling` for a robot that rolls on its base's wheels, `standing` or `mounted` for one that holds where it is placed. The release stands a robot rolling exactly when its description gives it a base |
| `linkBodies` | URDF link name to MJCF body name, where they differ, which is where each camera hangs |
| `ik.arms[].gains` | the MIT servo gains of each joint of the arm group `name`: its actuator becomes a position servo at `kp` with its damping raised to critical against the joint's inertia, as the real drivers run; an arm without gains runs its file's actuators |
| `ik.arms[].gripper.gains` | the position servo of each finger of the arm's gripper |
| `gravityCompensation` | counter-gravity on every body the model's joints move, as the real drivers feed gravity forward; a rolling robot rests its weight on its wheels and takes none |

The OpenArm arms run the real arm's gains, the defaults of `openarm/arm/peppy.json5`, and `tests/test_mujoco_models.py` holds them equal. The SO-101 runs MuJoCo Menagerie's STS3215 servo model. A joint the gains name must be driven by one actuator of the file, and a stand refuses one that none or several drive. `engine/models/<model>.json5` holds what this engine alone decides about the model:

| Key | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `camera_lights` | add the engine's light rig when rendering, for a scene that ships no light |
| `floor` | the robot works against a floor, which the scene lays once for the robots standing that ask for one and for every robot that rolls |

The engine stands `botshare_base`, `openarm_v1`, `openarm_v2` and `so101`. A model with no entry is refused naming these, and none falls back to another's layout. The engine's entries, `sim_robot_core`'s camera renders and the release's entry of each model the engine stands are read once at node setup, strictly: an entry that does not read, an unknown key, a field of the wrong type, or an entry of a model the release does not stand stops the node before a robot attaches as it, naming the file and the field. What depends on the robot's description is checked as it attaches, and a description that does not fit is refused with `rejected`: a camera the description has and no render names, or the reverse, and release gains that name an arm the description lacks or give an arm another number of joints than its group moves. `tests/test_release_models.py` holds each model of the release to its description.

To add a model: ship its description in `robot_description`, and its camera renders in `sim_robot_core` when it carries a camera; publish a release of Waldo's catalogue that stands it and pin it in Waldo's `web/public/assets/catalogue.lock.json` and in `sim_base_images/waldo_catalogue.py`; write `engine/models/<model>.json5`; then bump `MUJOCO_IMAGE_REV` and run `sim_base_images/build_base_images.sh`. [`sim_robot_core`'s README](https://github.com/Peppy-bot/public-peppy-libs/tree/main/sim_robot_core#adding-a-robot) lists the steps of both engines.

## Cameras

With `cameras_enabled`, the engine renders the cameras of every standing robot's own model, each on the pair of the relay named after it, and a model with no camera renders none. A camera hangs from the body its parent link is in the model's MJCF, at its optical frame, unless the MJCF defines a camera under its name: that camera is then the one rendered, where the file puts it, at the field of view of its render, and one that projects through a `sensorsize` of its own is refused. The botshare base's MJCF, written from its description, carries `base_camera` so, on the body that holds every link fixed joints join to its root, and `tests/test_release_models.py` holds it where the description puts the optical frame. Rendering is selected at launch: a launcher's rendered camera rig sets the parameter.

The cameras render through EGL, on the first device MuJoCo can open. Where the NVIDIA driver names the CUDA device behind each EGL device, MuJoCo tries those devices first: in the order of `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES` when the node's environment holds it, and in the order of their CUDA ids otherwise. `MUJOCO_EGL_DEVICE_ID` selects one device and no other is tried: its place in `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES` or its CUDA id, and where the driver names no CUDA device, its place in EGL's own order. Both variables reach the node from the environment of the `peppy` command that launches it, when the node runs on the machine of that command.

## Base image

The node builds on `peppybot/sim-mujoco`, which `sim_base_images/build_base_images.sh` produces for `linux/amd64` and `linux/arm64` with MuJoCo, `sim_robot_core`, `robot_description_py`, `control_core_py` and the pinned release of Waldo's catalogue baked in, read from the public store with no token. The script stamps the tag into `apptainer.def`.

`sim_base_images/requirements.mujoco.txt` pins the MuJoCo the image installs, and the script reads the version in the tag out of that file. To move the node to another MuJoCo, change the pin there and in `tests/pyproject.toml`, write the lock of the suites again and run the script. `tests/test_base_image.py` holds the tag, `apptainer.def` and the pins of the suites to that file.

## Tests

```sh
cd tests
uv run pytest
```

The suites render nothing. `test_base_physics.py` drives the botshare base as its base node drives it, straight, through a turn and at rest, and holds where it goes to the differential mix of its wheels' measured turns, within bounds its URDF's tires give. `test_base_physics.py`, `test_mujoco_models.py`, `test_release_models.py` and `test_robots_io.py` read the release from `PEPPY_ROBOT_ASSETS_DIR`, and where that directory holds none they stage the pinned release once in the machine's temporary directory, which needs the network on the first run. Every file is checked against the pin either way.
